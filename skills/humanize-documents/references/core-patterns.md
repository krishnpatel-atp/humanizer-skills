# Core patterns of machine-sounding writing

Shared reference for every humanizer skill. These are editing prompts, not bans. One tell on its own proves nothing; act when several appear together, or when a single strong one (marked ★) appears. Human writers use every one of these sometimes. The goal is writing a human reader trusts, not hiding where it came from.

Sources: Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup); blader/humanizer (MIT); Kobak et al. 2024 and Juzek & Ward 2024 on excess vocabulary in scientific abstracts. Full list in the repo README.

## A. Staging instead of stating

1. ★ **Manufactured contrast.** "It's not X, it's Y." "Not just X, but Y." "Y rather than X." "This doesn't mean X. It means Y." Fix: state Y. Keep the contrast only when someone actually believes X.
2. ★ **One-line closers.** "That's the real win." "Let that sink in." "And that changes everything." A paragraph that ends by restating itself as a slogan. Fix: delete the line; the paragraph already said it.
3. ★ **Fake-deep framing.** "At its core," "the real question is," "fundamentally," "the heart of the matter," "X is the currency of Y." Fix: say the point plainly.
4. ★ **Run-up openers.** "Let's dive in." "Here's the thing." "Here's what you need to know." "In today's fast-paced world." "Let's break it down." Fix: start with the first real sentence.
5. ★ **Arguing with no one.** "To be clear," "Don't get me wrong," "I'm not saying," "You might think… but." Fix: drop unless a real objection exists.

## B. Rhythm by rule

6. **Forced triads.** Lists of three by habit ("fast, flexible, and future-proof"). Fix: use the number of items the meaning has.
7. **Uniform sentences.** Every sentence 15–25 words, every paragraph 3–4 sentences, same opener repeated. Fix: vary length on purpose; merge or split.
8. **Dash as universal joint.** Em dashes everywhere where a comma, colon, period or parentheses fit. Fix: use the plainer mark; keep a dash only if the writer's own sample uses them.
9. **Stacked hedges.** "could potentially," "may arguably," "it's important to note that." Fix: one hedge, placed where the uncertainty is. Keep honest hedges.

## C. Inflation and borrowed authority

10. ★ **Stock AI vocabulary** (see word-choice list below). One word is coincidence; a cluster is a tell.
11. **Inflated significance.** "stands as a testament," "plays a pivotal role," "marks a turning point," "an enduring legacy," "the evolving landscape." Fix: say what happened and let the reader judge.
12. **Shallow -ing tails.** "…, highlighting the importance of X." "…, ensuring Y." "…, fostering Z." Fix: cut, or make it a real clause with evidence.
13. **Sales copy.** "nestled," "vibrant," "breathtaking," "rich tapestry," "seamless," "cutting-edge," "game-changer." Fix: concrete facts.
14. **Borrowed authority.** "Experts say," "studies show," "industry reports suggest," with nothing cited. Fix: cite the source or drop the claim.
15. **Avoiding "is" and "has."** "serves as," "stands as," "boasts," "features," "functions as." Fix: is / are / has.
16. **Vague association.** "is associated with," "is linked to," "is tied to." Fix: name the actual relationship.

## D. Formatting by rule

17. ★ **Bold-label bullets.** Every bullet starts with **Bold Phrase:** then a sentence. Fix: plain bullets, or prose.
18. **Decorative headings.** Title Case On Every Word, emoji in headings, a horizontal rule between every section, a heading that repeats the title. Fix: sentence case, fewer headings.
19. **Emoji as bullets or decoration.** ✅ 🚀 💡 🔑 in professional text. Fix: remove unless the genre uses them.
20. **Headers on short content.** A 150-word answer split into four sections. Fix: write paragraphs.

## E. Chat residue

21. ★ **Assistant talk left in.** "Certainly!", "Great question!", "I hope this helps," "Let me know if you'd like me to…", "Here is a revised version:", "As an AI language model." Fix: delete. Strongest single tell there is.
22. ★ **Knowledge-limit hedges.** "As of my last update," "specific details are not widely documented," "based on available information." Fix: find the fact or remove the sentence.
23. ★ **Placeholders.** "[Your Name]", "[Insert statistic]", "2025-XX-XX", "Company X". Fix: fill in from the user, or ask.
24. **Writing about the document.** "This section outlines…", "The table below compares…", "In this essay, I will…". Fix: just do it.
25. **Re-explaining what the reader knows.** Restating the question or the problem before answering. Fix: lead with the answer.

## Universal rewrite rules

- Keep every fact, number, name, date, quote, citation and caveat. Never invent details, sources, anecdotes or data to sound "more human."
- If the text needs a concrete detail it doesn't have, ask for it or write a simpler sentence. Do not make one up.
- Match the writer's own voice when you have a sample. The sample beats these rules.
- Don't flatten deliberate style. Informal, angry, funny or quirky writing is usually human; leave its register alone.
- Text written before late 2022 was not written by a chatbot. Don't "fix" it for that reason.
- Never promise the result will pass an AI detector. Detectors have real error rates, and that isn't the goal.
- Watched words inside quotations, titles, names, code or technical terms (e.g., "robust estimator," "landscape orientation") are not tells.
