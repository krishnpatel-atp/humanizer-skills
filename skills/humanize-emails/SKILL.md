# Humanize emails and messages

People spot generated email by tone before vocabulary: smooth, formal, generic, padded at both ends, formatted like a report. Style alone is weak evidence (plenty of humans write this way), but it still costs attention and trust. Also read `references/core-patterns.md`.

## The shape of a human email

1. **Subject line** that says what's inside or what's needed: "Need sign-off on Q1 budget by Fri," not "Following Up" or "Quick Question."
2. **First line = the point.** The ask, the answer, or the news.
3. **Only the context needed** to act on it. Usually 1–3 short paragraphs.
4. **Clear next step** with a date if there is one.
5. **Sign-off** the writer actually uses ("Thanks," "Best," "Cheers," or just a name).

Match the relationship: a note to a close colleague can be two lines with no greeting. A first message to a stranger is more formal but still short.

## Tells and fixes

| Tell | Fix |
|---|---|
| "I hope this email finds you well" / "I hope you're doing well" as automatic opener | Cut, or use a real personal line if the writer has one |
| "I wanted to reach out to…" / "I am writing to…" | Start with what you're asking or telling |
| "Just circling back / following up on my previous email" with no new info | Restate the ask in one line with the deadline |
| ★ Bold headings, bullet lists, or "**Key Points:**" in a short email | Plain sentences. Bullets only for 3+ separate items the reader must act on |
| ★ Placeholders: "[Your Name]", "[Company]", "[specific detail]" | Fill from the user or ask |
| Over-thanking and over-apologizing ("Thank you so much for your time and consideration; I truly appreciate…") | One thanks, where it means something |
| "Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions" / "Let me know if you need anything else" | Cut, or make specific: "If Tuesday doesn't work, suggest a time." |
| Stock enthusiasm ("I'm thrilled," "excited to explore synergies") | Plain interest: "I'd like to talk about X." |
| Three balanced paragraphs every time | Length follows content; two lines is fine |
| AI vocabulary ("delve," "leverage," "streamline," "align," "valuable insights") | Plain words (see word list) |
| "It's not just about X, it's about Y" | Say Y |
| Restating the other person's email back to them | Reply to the question |
| Overly formal for the relationship ("Dear Ms. Rivera" to a teammate) | Match how they wrote to you |

## Cover letters and cold outreach

- Open with the specific reason for writing this person or company, not "I am excited to apply for the position of…"
- One or two real, specific examples from the user's own experience. **Never invent accomplishments, numbers, employers or mutual contacts.** If none are provided, ask.
- Cut "I am a highly motivated, detail-oriented team player passionate about…"
- Under ~250 words for a cover letter unless the user wants more; under ~120 for cold outreach.

## Replies and chat messages

- Answer the question in the first line.
- Slack/Teams: no greeting paragraph, no sign-off, no headers. Lowercase and fragments are fine if the writer writes that way.
- Keep the writer's quirks (contractions, emoji they actually use, "haha"). Removing all personality is its own tell.

## Workflow

1. Find the one thing the recipient must do or know. Put it first.
2. Cut the opener, the closer filler and any repeated context.
3. Check names, dates, times, amounts, links and attachments mentioned are unchanged.
4. Offer a subject line if it's a new email.
5. Return the email ready to paste; don't send anything on the user's behalf.
