# Overused-word list and plain replacements

Shared reference. A single word from this list means nothing. Many of them, close together, in text written after late 2022, is one of the strongest signs of unedited model output (Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing"). Replace by meaning, not by synonym: swapping "delve" for "dig deep" keeps the problem.

Research basis: Kobak et al. (2024) measured "excess vocabulary" in ~14M PubMed abstracts and estimated at least 10% of 2024 abstracts were LLM-processed. Juzek & Ward (2024) documented the jump in "delve," "intricate" and "nuanced." Kousha (2025) found "delve" (+1,500%), "underscore" (+1,000%) and "intricate" (+700%) rising after ChatGPT's release. Language Log notes "delve" was already rising before 2022, so treat the list as a signal, not proof.

## Words by model era (rough; not hard cutoffs)

| Era | Words that clustered |
|---|---|
| 2023 – mid-2024 | additionally, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, garner, intricate/intricacies, interplay, key, landscape, meticulously, pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant |
| mid-2024 – mid-2025 | align with, bolstered, crucial, emphasizing, enhance, enduring, fostering, highlighting, pivotal, showcasing, underscore, vibrant |
| mid-2025 – mid-2026 | emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing; plus media-coverage phrasing ("featured in trade publications") |
| mid-2026 onward | challenge, clearer, dependable, echoed, foster, leverage, matters, multifaceted, practical, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally |

Source: era table in Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing," as of late 2026.

## Phrases to cut outright

"In today's [fast-paced / digital] world" · "It's worth noting that" · "When it comes to" · "plays a [key/crucial/pivotal] role in" · "a testament to" · "the [evolving] landscape of" · "at the end of the day" · "navigate the complexities of" · "a deep dive into" · "the power of" · "in the realm of" · "embark on a journey" · "stay ahead of the curve" · "I hope this helps" · "Let me know if…"

## Don't over-correct

- Technical senses are fine: "robust standard errors," "key-value store," "landscape orientation," "underscore character," "a crucial point" when it really is.
- Don't replace every flagged word. One "crucial" in a 2,000-word report is normal English.
- Don't swap one tic for another. If every "crucial" becomes "critical," you've changed nothing.
